# Wearable watch: decisions

The design decisions taken while building the watch, numbered so that the
source can cite them (`D-Wn`). The open questions they settle are in
[open-questions.md](open-questions.md) (`OQ-Wn`). The last section explains
every diagnostic that `protoboard check` still reports.

## D-W1 Board: a 41 mm, 4-layer, 0.8 mm disc

The board is a 41 mm disc under the 1.28-inch round display. It is 0.8 mm
thick, has 4 layers and an ENIG finish, and it is assembled by the fab
(`src/boards/watch-main/circuit.config.ts`).

- **Four layers.** The board has 47 components and only routes with 4
  layers. Raytac asks for an unbroken ground under the module's pads (§2.3).
- **Vias of 0.3 mm with a 0.15 mm drill.** Larger vias land within 0.1 mm of
  the 0.4 mm-pitch pads. Confirm the drill with the fab.
- **Placements fixed by the case.** USB-C at 9 o'clock, seen from the back.
  The buttons are radial at 150° and 210° from the cavity centre, so their
  plungers cut square through the round wall. The heart-rate sensor sits
  under the back window. The BLE module's antenna end is at the board edge.
- **Hand layout.** All other parts are placed by hand in
  `tscircuit/layout.json`. The router then reaches 0 DRC errors.

## D-W2 The display is a component of the board (OQ-W1, OQ-W5)

The ER-TFT1.28-1 has a 12-pin solder-tab FPC with a 0.7 mm pitch. It is
therefore part of the board (`ds1`):

- It sits on the bottom side, rotated 180°. Its footprint origin is the FPC
  contact frame.
- The FPC drops 4.65 mm past the cell (D-W15). It is soldered to a land with the
  datasheet's "hollow out" pattern, as a post-reflow hand step.
- The board's GLB, STEP and manifest carry the display. The viewer therefore
  shows it in place, and the case's display window serves `board:display_face`.

Stack order: display / cell / board, as in OQ-W5's proposed answer
([harnesses-and-cad.md](harnesses-and-cad.md#stack-up)).

## D-W3 Passives by value on the board

The resistors and capacitors are catalog part families (`yageo-rc0402`,
`murata-grm155`, `murata-grm188`). Each placement chooses its value with
`overrides` in `watch-main.uhd.ts`. The BOM groups them by value, and the
release lists the value of every placement.

The 20 pF C0G load capacitors for the 12.5 pF crystal (OQ-W9) were not in
the catalog. That part was researched into `parts/local`
(`murata-grm1555c1h200ja01`). Its evidence is in `parts/evidence/`.

## D-W4 Power: linear regulators, charger on VBAT (OQ-W2, OQ-W3)

- **Rails.** 3V0 (TPS7A0230) supplies the module and the display. 1V8
  (TPS7A0218) supplies the MAX30102 core.
- **Charger.** The MCP73831 charges at 83 mA (RPROG 12 kΩ), with the system
  load on VBAT. There is no power path for v0.1.
- **Peak current.** The cell pads state the instantaneous peak drawn with the
  firmware limits, 177 mA (the backlight counts at its 39 mA on-current, not
  its PWM average). Without those limits the peak is about 254 mA.

The battery-life targets are checked in
[power-budget.md](power-budget.md#checked-against-the-brief).

## D-W5 Case: printed PETG, round, fixed cavity (OQ-W11, OQ-W12)

The case is generated by the cad capability. It is built from its features:

- the display window, with a 38 × 0.5 mm PMMA lens;
- the heart-rate window, with a 4.0 × 8.2 × 0.5 mm PMMA cover;
- the USB-C opening;
- two button plungers;
- 22 mm strap lugs;
- the back cover, with four M2 × 6 screws into ruthex M2 inserts.

The cavity is fixed in `cad.config.ts` rather than sized automatically:

- **Radius 21.26 mm, centre 0.5 mm towards +x.** This keeps the USB-C mouth
  1.83 mm behind the outer surface. The plug's overmold reaches 1.85 mm.
- **Automatic sizing would not fit.** It centres the cavity on the contents
  and grows it to 21.5 mm, which leaves the receptacle 2.5 mm deep.
- **Walls 1.1 mm, floor and lid 1.3 mm.** The case is 13.49 mm thick,
  against the brief's 13.5 mm.

## D-W6 Retention: bonded parts, screwed back cover

Nothing screws the board, the cell or the LRA to the case. Their positions
are in `.protoboard/display.json`:

- The board and its display are held by the lens pocket and the walls. The
  display is bonded to the floor around the window. The adhesive is not yet
  chosen.
- The cell sits on a 0.25 mm foam pad between the display and the board,
  with 0.53 mm left to the board for it to swell (D-W15).
- The LRA is bonded can-first to the inside of the back cover with a
  0.25 mm adhesive pad. This couples its vibration to the wrist.
- The display is held on the case floor by a 0.25 mm adhesive ring outside
  its visual area, and the two PMMA covers by a bead of UV-curing adhesive at
  their rims.

These materials are BOM lines (`src/materials.uhd.ts`), specified by
thickness and kind; products are open (OQ-W17).

The back cover is closed by `watch-back-cover-screws`: four M2 × 6 A2 pan-head
screws into heat-set inserts in the base. The case's `back_joint` links to
itself through that harness (link `back_cover`), and the harness's
`fastenerStack` places the inserts and screws at the four columns the case
provider reports, so the assembly guide has a step that presses the inserts
and closes the cover, with its torque (0.1 N·m, an assumption).

## D-W7 Leads instead of connectors

The cell and the LRA keep their own leads. The leads are cut to length and
soldered to pads J3 and J4. The harnesses `watch-battery-leads` (7.5 mm
installed) and `watch-lra-leads` (6 mm) carry those links, so UHD derives the
power links through them. ext-harness-cad routes their geometry inside the
cavity, with the slack laid out at the 2.5 mm minimum bend radius. There is
no room for connectors at this height.

The lengths follow the assembly order (the assembly guide's steps):

1. The display's FPC is soldered to its land on the board's rear face with the
   display lying unfolded beside the board's +x edge (board unit, D-W2).
2. The cell lies beside the board's +y edge, PCM end towards J3 and up to about
   3 mm past the edge; its leads (cut to 9 mm, 1.5 mm stripped) are soldered
   to J3. The cell then folds under the board's rear face onto its foam pad.
3. The display folds over the cell on its FPC.
4. The LRA lies on the board's free region while its leads (cut to 7.5 mm)
   are soldered to J4; it is bonded to the back cover as the cover closes.

Longer leads would give more working slack but no longer fit: harness-cad
reports the slack "not clear" from 8 mm for either cable.

## D-W8 Antenna clearance (OQ-W13)

The circuit config keeps every layer clear between the module's antenna end
and the board edge (the `antenna` keepout, 14 × 1.8 mm).

The router does not treat the area under the module's antenna as a keepout.
The routed board is therefore checked separately with
`npx tsx src/boards/watch-main/antenna-check.ts`. It looks for traces, vias
and pads in the module's last 3.8 mm beyond its pads and on to the board
edge, across 14 mm. Result: none.

How close the cell and the display's metal frame come to the antenna end is
not yet checked. No RF test is part of v0.1.

## D-W9 LRA drive range

The DRV2605L can drive up to VDD. The J4 pads state the VG0832012's range
instead: 1.8 Vrms rated, 1.85 Vrms maximum. Firmware sets `RATED_VOLTAGE`
and `OD_CLAMP` to match. In `firmware.config.ts`, `actuator-mode` is set to
`LRA`, because the Zephyr binding requires it and the part's facts do not
choose it.

## D-W10 SWD on test pads

Programming uses five passive pads (J5) on a 1.27 mm pitch. A spring-pin clip
reaches them with the back cover off. A programmer is not part of the
product, so the pads state nothing about what is attached. For the same
reason, the firmware check reports the SWD nets as unused (see below).

## D-W11 Firmware: Zephyr 4.2 board definition

ext-zephyr generates the Zephyr board definition, `wearable_watch`, and the
Kconfig fragment from the board. Every line cites the board's nets and parts
(`src/boards/watch-main/zephyr/`, `out/firmware.manifest.json`).

- **Wake sources.** Both buttons and the IMU interrupt wake the watch from
  System OFF.
- **Sensors without drivers.** The BMI270 (u2) and MAX30102 (u3) have no
  driver in the provider's table. Their nodes carry labels, and the
  application drives them itself. Zephyr has upstream drivers for both
  (`bosch,bmi270`, `maxim,max30101`, which the MAX30102 is register-compatible
  with). Adding them to ext-zephyr's table is a follow-up.

## D-W12 USB-C plug seat on a round wall

The receptacle's mouth is a flat face, and the case wall is round. The cad
check measures how the USB-C plug's overmold seats on the case's outer
surface and gives two warnings. They were accepted for the first v0.1 build:

- **The curved wall leaves 0.87 mm under the ends of the overmold.** The
  plug's 12.35 mm wide overmold meets a 22.36 mm-radius wall, which falls
  away from it by 0.87 mm at its ends.
- **The overmold overhangs the flat face around the opening by 0.12 mm.**

Effect: the plug seats on the middle of its face. The contacts still mate,
because the mouth is 1.83 mm deep against the plug's 1.85 mm reach. The plug
can rock slightly.

**Fixed in review.** ext-enclosure-cad's `Opening` takes `seat: "flat"`: it
adds material outside the round wall up to the plane through the opening's
outermost point, over the overmold's face plus 0.3 mm a side (12.95 × 7.1
mm, standing up to 0.87 mm proud of the curve at its ends). The plug now
bears on a flat face, the wall keeps its 1.1 mm, and the case is no wider
along the plug's axis. The seat is split at the joint between the base and
the back cover.

The overmold still reaches 0.12 mm past the back face of the case. It seats
on the face and overhangs the edge into air. The check reports such an
overhang as an info only beyond its 0.3 mm alignment tolerance, so it is
silent here.

## D-W13 Strap

The case has 22 mm lugs, each with a spring bar (Esslinger 82.2225, `esslinger-82-2225-spring-bar`,
`bar_12` and `bar_6`). The strap itself is user-supplied and is not modelled
(OQ-W15).

## D-W14 Faults the checks catch

`src/scenarios.ts` holds seven system scenarios: nominal, plus six faults (the sixth, `stat-overdrive`, D-W17). A
UHD rule catches each fault (`system-compose` scenarios script).

The firmware side was checked by hand, by applying each fault to the
sources and running `protoboard check`:

| Fault | Caught by |
| --- | --- |
| SDA and SCL swapped at the fuel gauge (u5) | `FW_BUS_CROSSED` (and the circuit's `CIRCUIT_NET_SHORT` and `CIRCUIT_NET_OPEN` on the routed board) |
| A wake source that is not an input of the board (`sw3`) | `FW_WAKE_UNKNOWN` |
| `actuator-mode` left out for the DRV2605L | `FW_PROPERTY_MISSING` |
| Any model change after `firmware:generate` | `FW_NATIVE_STALE` |

## D-W15 Room for the cell to swell (OQ-W16)

A LiPo pouch grows over its life; the LP402030's sources state no swelling
allowance. The first build left 0.28 mm between the cell and the board. The
display's FPC now drops 4.65 mm instead of 4.4 mm (the display part's
installed state), which moves the board 0.25 mm towards the back cover:

- cell on its 0.25 mm foam pad on the display's rear face (z 1.75 to 5.75);
- 0.53 mm to the board's rear face (z 6.28), 13 % of the cell's thickness,
  plus whatever the foam pad gives;
- the cavity's top follows (10.64), and the case is 13.49 mm thick, inside
  the brief's 13.5 mm.

The FPC keeps its path length, so its contacts move 0.25 mm outwards: the
board's display land moved with them (`ds1` at x 15.41).

## D-W16 Window covers and assembly materials

The display lens (38 × 0.5 mm) and the heart-rate cover (4.0 × 8.2 ×
0.5 mm) are parts of their own (`src/enclosures/watch-case/glazing.uhd.ts`),
laser cut from clear PMMA. ext-enclosure-cad builds each outside window's
cover and writes its STEP, GLB and a DXF outline for cutting; each cover
mates flush with its window (links `lens_seat`, `hr_cover_seat`), so the
assembly guide places it in a step and the BOM and the release carry it.
The tapes and the adhesive are BOM lines too (D-W6).

## D-W17 Charger status through a divider

The MCP73831's STAT output is tri-state, not open drain: high at charge
complete is its VDD, which is VBUS (5 V). Wired straight to P0.08 it would
drive a 3.0 V GPIO above its VDD + 0.3 V limit whenever charging finished
with USB plugged in. R10/R11 (22 kΩ / 22 kΩ) halve it to 2.4–2.6 V, above the
pin's VIH (2.1 V); low stays under 0.1 V. High-Z (no cell, shutdown) reads
low through R11, so the firmware reads STAT only while USBD reports VBUS.

UHD's net rule now catches the original wiring: a push-pull output on a
single-supply part drives its net up to that supply, and an input rated
below it is an error (scenario `stat-overdrive`).

The MAX17048's CELL pin, not connected inside the part, is tied to VBAT as
its application circuit shows.

## D-W18 Ground planes on the inner layers

Both inner layers are ground pours (`pours` in `circuit.config.ts`), filled
after routing around the other nets. They stay out of every keep-out on
every layer: the antenna strip at the board edge and, on the inner layers,
the module's antenna end beyond its pads (`antenna_inner`). The pours are
written to the inner-layer Gerbers, and `antenna-check.ts` checks that no
pour reaches the antenna zone.

## D-W19 Land patterns verified against the makers' drawings

Every land pattern on the board is the maker's recommended one, from
ext-tscircuit's table, with a verification record (document, page, figure
and the drawing's values), and `circuit:check` reports none unverified:
Raytac MDBT50Q-1MV2 Ver. L §2.2 (all 61 pad centres), Bosch BMI270 §8.3,
Maxim 90-0602 (MAX30102) and 90-0065 (MAX17048), TI DGS0010A and DBV0005A,
Microchip C04-2091-OT, ST DS4260 Figure 19, AOS PO-00001, Epson FC-135,
Alps SKRT Drawing No.1, GCT USB4105 rev B4, the display's FPC contacts
(EastRising gives no board land), Yageo and Murata reflow lands.
`tscircuit/footprints.json` keeps only the lead and SWD pads, citing their
pad parts.

- **USB4105.** The stake slots are GCT's (0.60 × 1.40 and 0.60 × 1.70 mm in
  1.00 × 1.80 and 1.00 × 2.10 mm rings; they were 1.0 and 1.3 mm long), the
  tail lands sit on GCT's tail line, and the board edge is where GCT puts it,
  2.60 mm in front of the front slots: the outline is the Ø41 mm disc with a
  flat at x = -20.02 (the disc left a 0.48 mm lip under the mouth that the
  plug's overmold would meet).
- **Switches.** Alps' land has Ø0.9 mm guide-boss holes (not drilled
  before) and larger fixing-leg lands. Turned 240° and 300°, the switches'
  bounding boxes cross the disc although every pad and the courtyard is
  inside; `board.tsx` lets them (and the receptacle, whose face is on the
  edge) off the board for tscircuit's box test only.
- **Layout.** With IPC-7351B courtyards, Y1, C7, D1, R1, R5 and R11 moved
  0.05-0.25 mm. The board re-routed with 0 errors (4 layers, 129 vias, none in
  a pad).
- **Fabrication files.** The stencil is the land patterns' own (it was
  tscircuit's 70 % per side), the silkscreen carries references and pin-1
  marks, and the switches' turned paste apertures are written as regions.
