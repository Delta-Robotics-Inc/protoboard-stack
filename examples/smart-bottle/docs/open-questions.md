# Smart water bottle: open questions

Decisions and unknowns handed to later tracks. Each has a proposed answer so a
track can proceed; the owner confirms or overrides. Source tags refer to the
[component selection sources](component-selection.md#sources).

| ID | Question | Proposed answer | Owner track | Blocks |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| OQ-B1 | Does time of flight read a water surface reliably from the cap, through a window, over a cold drink? Risks: specular reflection from a calm surface, ripples, condensation fogging the window, crosstalk from the window. | Treat level sensing as unproven. Window: clear PMMA or glass, IR-transmissive at 940 nm [B3], as thin as practical and close to the sensor, with ST's crosstalk calibration [B3] §2.3. Add a hydrophobic or anti-fog treatment (not selected). Firmware takes the median of several ranges and ignores readings while the bottle is moving. Validate on the first physical build; until then mark level data as stand-in. | parts research, enclosure, firmware | Window design |
| OQ-B2 | The probe hangs from the cap, so in an upright bottle that is not full it measures headspace air, not water. | Accept for v0.1: in a closed bottle the headspace tracks the water temperature after it settles (**Assumption**, untested), and the probe is wetted whenever the bottle is full or tipped. Alternatives for later: a longer stalk reaching the lower third of the bottle (awkward when the cap is off), or a probe in the bottle base. | enclosure, firmware | Probe boss |
| OQ-B3 | Buck-boost: keep the library's TPS63020 (25 + 5 µA quiescent [B8]) or move to the TPS63802 (11 µA [B9])? Also: keep U4 always on, or switch it off between samples? | Keep the TPS63020 for v0.1 to exercise a catalog part; the budget meets its target with it. Move to the TPS63802 if a smaller cell is chosen (OQ-B10) or the sleep floor matters. Keep U4 always on: switching it off needs a wake path that does not depend on the ESP32-C3. | board | Regulator footprint |
| OQ-B4 | The bottle's thread. Nalgene names a 63 mm closure with semi-buttress threads [B17, B20]; a Nalgene packaging bottle is listed as 63-415 [B19]; the SPI table gives 63 mm T, E, S and pitch but no 415-series H [B21]. | Measure the purchased bottle (T, E, H, S, I, pitch, thread start and profile) before the cap thread is frozen; record the measurements as evidence on `nalgene-32oz-wide-mouth`. Until then use the SPI 63 mm values and a 4.233 mm pitch, marked as assumptions on the part. The cap must carry 56 in-lb (6.3 N·m) closing torque [B20]. Also design how the printed wall seals around the IP67 USB-C receptacle (gasket face per GCT's drawing, not yet read). | enclosure | Cap thread, USB opening |
| OQ-B5 | Food contact. Printed PETG or PLA, the PVC probe cable, the window and the O-rings face drinking water or its vapour. | Keep the PVC cable inside the sealed compartment (only the stainless tube enters the bottle). Use FDA-grade silicone for the lip and probe O-rings. Choose a cap material with a food-contact declaration (for example a food-contact PETG filament or an SLA resin with such a declaration); none is selected or claimed in v0.1. | enclosure | Material choice |
| OQ-B6 | Antenna. The ESP32-C3-MINI-1's PCB antenna sits in a cap above water and next to a 34 × 50 mm cell; water absorbs 2.4 GHz. | Place the module at the board rim with its antenna end up and outward, keep the cell and copper out of the keep-out [B1], and keep metal out of the lid. Fallback: ESP32-C3-MINI-1U with an external antenna on the lid [B1]. No RF test in v0.1. | board, enclosure | Placement |
| OQ-B7 | LED ring at low battery and the light guide. Blue and green need up to 3.1 V [B7] plus 0.25–0.35 V at the driver [B6]; the cell is down to 3.40 V at cut-off. | Drive 2–5 mA per channel and stop using blue and green below about 3.5 V (fuel gauge). Light guide: a translucent printed ring in the lid over the 8 LEDs, or a separate clear part; the enclosure track decides. Confirm common-anode pinout on the Kingbright drawing [B7]. Stock of APTF1616LSEEZGKQBKC at JLCPCB is 305 [B26]; find a second source before production. | parts research, board, enclosure | LED footprint, lid |
| OQ-B8 | The MAX17048 fuel gauge is not in the agreed defaults. | Keep it: an accurate state of charge at 4 µA, no divider loading the cell, and the same part as the watch [B11]. Drop it only if the owner wants the smallest BOM (a switched divider on an ADC pin would replace it). | owner | — |
| OQ-B9 | Sealed button. A side-push switch [B15] pressed through the cap wall needs a seal. | A printed plunger behind a thin flexible membrane (printed TPU or a moulded silicone boot); the enclosure track chooses and checks the 1.6 N actuation force through the membrane. | enclosure | Cap wall |
| OQ-B10 | The 1000 mAh cell is about three times the target profile's need (90 days estimated against a 30-day target). | Keep it for v0.1 (data in hand, fits the cap). A 500 mAh cell would still give about 45 days in the target profile; choose one with a datasheet if the cap gets smaller or lighter. | owner, parts research | Cap height |
| OQ-B11 | Does the charger need a power path? The ESP32-C3's Wi-Fi bursts (up to 350 mA [B1]) during charging share the charger's 303 mA output. | Not for v0.1: the firmware pauses Wi-Fi sync while charging and syncs after. Alternative: a charger with power path. Decide with the watch's OQ-W3 so both projects use the same answer. | board, firmware | Charger choice |
| OQ-B12 | Strapping pins carry the I2C bus (GPIO2 = SCL, GPIO8 = SDA). A device holding SDA low at reset would turn GPIO8 low. | Accept: SPI boot ignores GPIO8 [B1] Table 4-3, so normal boot is unaffected; only download mode needs GPIO8 high, and the bus is idle then. | board | — |
| OQ-B13 | ESP32-C3-MINI-1-H4X (−40 to 105 °C, in JLCPCB stock) or -N4X (−40 to 85 °C, not seen in JLCPCB) [B1, B26]? | H4X for availability; they share a footprint, so the choice can change at ordering. | parts research | — |
| OQ-B14 | VL53L4CD datasheet revision. The copy read is Rev 5; the current revision is Rev 8 [B3]. DS18B20: an older Dallas revision was read [B5]. | Re-check every cited value against the current revisions in the parts-research track. | parts research | — |
| OQ-B16 | The bayonet lid's printed fit (D-B09): 0.2 mm radial and 0.15 mm axial clearance at the lugs, 1.0 mm lugs (one a 10 mm key), no detent. The tightest fit is not at the lugs but at the seal: the skirt's bore slides over the Ø76.0 collar with Parker's 0.089 mm diametral clearance once printed, for which the bore is drawn 0.1 mm (radial) oversize; a bore that prints smaller binds, one that prints at its drawn Ø76.29 squeezes the O-ring only 13.5–29.7 % with the collar off centre (22–32 % wanted). Two walls are at or near the limit on a part twisted against the O-ring's friction: the lid's skirt behind the bayonet slots (0.8 mm, two perimeters, the minimum) and the base wall behind the collar's O-ring groove (1.05 mm). Does it go on and turn by hand, hold the lid against the O-ring's push, stay turned, and do those walls hold? | Print the base and the lid first; measure the skirt's bore (it should be Ø76.09 ± 0.05) and try them before printing the rest. Adjust `boreAllowanceMm` to the bore measured, `clearanceMm`, `axialClearanceMm` or the lug size in the cap module and regenerate; if a thin wall cracks, raise `min_wall_mm` (cad.config.ts) and regenerate. | cap | Cap print |
| OQ-B15 | USB4720-03-A is not in the JLCPCB catalog [B26]; distributor stock was not checked. | Confirm at a distributor; if unavailable, the USB4730 (IP67 with a separate gasket) [B13b] is the nearest alternative. | parts research | J1 footprint |
