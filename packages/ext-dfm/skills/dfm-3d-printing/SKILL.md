# Design for 3D printing

The capability of the machine dictates the design. An FDM printer lays beads
about one nozzle wide and one layer tall, bonds them weakly across layers, and
cannot print in mid-air; so every wall, gap, hole, overhang and fit is sized in
nozzle widths and layer heights, the part is oriented before its details are
drawn, and supports are designed out rather than added later. The numbers are
the house profile (`profiles/fdm.json`: a tuned printer with a 0.4 mm
nozzle, 0.2 mm layers, PLA; no printer model is assumed until a layer names
one), written as formulas so they recompute for another nozzle. They are recommendations and defaults, never hard blocks: anything the
user states (a tested clearance, a material, a printer) overrides them, and the
agent says which values it assumed.

The rules that matter most:

1. **Choose the orientation first**, from the load, the cosmetic face and the
   bed contact. Layers are about half as strong across as along.
2. **Walls in whole line widths** (0.45 mm each): 0.9 mm minimum structural,
   1.35 to 1.8 mm for load paths. Nothing under one line width.
3. **No supports by design:** 45 deg maximum overhang from vertical, chamfers
   not fillets on downward edges, teardrop tops on horizontal holes, bridges
   under 10 mm (5 mm on show faces), split the part when nothing else works.
   A support that cannot be designed out is modeled in CAD with a stated gap
   (0.2 mm), never left to the slicer.
4. **Every clearance per side AND diametral.** House sliding fit: 0.20 mm per
   side = 0.40 mm diametral; 0.30 per side for an unknown printer. State the
   one-line clearance rule each time one is applied.
5. **Printed holes come out small:** add 0.2 mm diametral where a hole must
   finish at a stated size; never stack slicer hole compensation on top.
6. **Chamfer every bed-contact edge** (0.5 mm x 45 deg) against elephant's foot.
7. **Metal hardware for threads and magnets:** heat-set inserts (self-tapping
   inserts in PETG, PC, ABS) in a boss of solid perimeters, captured nuts
   (square in slots), pocketed magnets; no modeled threads under M5, M6 and up
   preferred.
8. **Prove what is not proven** with a coupon or a printed slice before a long
   print.
9. **Write down the print assumptions** (orientation, material, nozzle, layer,
   supports, pauses) for the person who slices it.
10. **A production run is its own mode:** printed in quantity or on a farm,
    the part shapes its bed contact for release and models its own brims,
    rafts and supports ([production runs](references/production-runs.md)).

```
resolved DFM profile (dfm) ──► process, printer, nozzle, layer, material
        ▼
orientation ─► walls ─► supports out ─► holes and fits ─► hardware ─► material
        ▼
bed contact ─► small features ─► split and join ─► coupons or slice
        ▼
print assumptions written ──► dfm-review
```

## Purpose

Shape a part so it prints right the first time on the target printer: strong
in the loaded direction, no generated supports, fits that fit, hardware that
holds, and a written record of the print assumptions it depends on. Use it
when a part will be 3D printed, when its printed geometry, material or printer
changes, or when a print failed and the cause may be the design. FDM is
covered in depth; SLA, SLS, MJF, metal and the rest are a reference so FDM rules
are not applied to them. Do not use it to pick between printing and another
process; that is **dfm**, which also resolves the profile this skill reads.
Cases around a board are generated by **enclosure-cad** and routed or machined
plates by **frame-cad**; apply this playbook to their printed parts. Styling
(edge vocabulary, texture, color) is **cad-style** and **style-system**. In
**build-a-project** it applies wherever a printed part is designed: after the
**dfm** skill has chosen the process, and before **dfm-review** checks the
result.

## Steps

### 1. Confirm the process, printer and print settings

Read the resolved profile from **dfm**. Confirm, and state in one line each:

- process (`fdm` unless the profile says otherwise; for SLA, SLS, MJF, metal or
  others switch to [other processes](references/other-processes.md) and do not
  use the FDM values below);
- printer (`machine.family`; a named Bambu model sets build volume and
  enclosure from [Bambu Lab](references/bambu-lab.md));
- nozzle (`machine.nozzle`, house 0.4), line width (`line.width`, 0.45 =
  1.125 x nozzle), layer height (`layer.height`, house 0.2), material
  (`material.default`, house PLA);
- which of these were assumed rather than stated. Ask only when the answer
  changes the design ([Bambu Lab](references/bambu-lab.md) section 7).

### 2. Choose the print orientation before modeling details

Decide, in this order, and record the result:

1. **Load direction.** Put the main load along the layers, never pulling them
   apart; the highest-stress region must not sit on a layer boundary. Assume
   across-layer strength is `strength.z.fraction` (0.5 for PLA; ASA 0.3 on an
   open printer) of in-layer strength, and across-layer impact strength only
   `strength.z.impact.fraction` (0.25): impact and drop loads never cross the
   layers.
2. **Cosmetic face.** The best faces are vertical walls and the bed face on a
   textured plate; shallow tops terrace.
3. **Bed contact.** A large flat face down; no point or edge contact (give it a
   1 mm chamfer pad, 2 mm `landing.edge.width` for tall parts); the first
   layer on a hidden or bottom face. A production run shapes and reduces
   contact for release instead ([production runs](references/production-runs.md)
   section 2).

If the arms of a part load in different directions, tilt at least 45 deg
(`orientation.tilt.min`) or split it (step 10). Model in the print orientation
and export in it. Rules: [FDM rules](references/fdm-rules.md) section 5.

### 3. Set walls and features in whole line widths

- Walls are multiples of `wall.unit` (0.45): 0.45 prints, 0.9 is the
  structural floor (`wall.structural`), 1.35 to 1.8 for load paths
  (`wall.loadbearing`), 1.2 when the printer is unknown.
- Never model a wall under one line width. A 0.2 mm wall, fine in some machined
  materials, is half a 0.4 mm nozzle: the Classic wall generator drops it, and
  Arachne widens it to a 0.34 mm bead, 70 percent oversize. Technically
  sliceable, wrong either way ([FDM rules](references/fdm-rules.md) section
  1.1).
- Strength comes from perimeters, not infill: plan for `perimeters.structural`
  (3) and design solid shapes; do not copy molding-style thin ribbed shells or
  draft.
- Ribs, where used: whole multiples of the line width, at least two lines, in
  the XY plane, with self-supporting undersides (FDM-STR-11).
- Blunt every modeled knife edge, blade or tip to a flat at least one line
  width wide; a true point thins the flow and flakes (FDM-WALL-9).
- Keep layer height at or under half the nozzle for strong parts
  (`layer.max.structural`).

### 4. Remove supports by design

Work through the overhangs in the chosen orientation:

- Keep overhangs at or under `overhang.max.angle` (45 deg from vertical). The
  limit is a step per layer of about half a line width, so thicker layers
  allow less.
- Chamfer under ledges and on downward-facing edges; never fillet them.
- Horizontal round holes get a 45 deg teardrop or roof; holes starting in
  mid-air (a counterbore from below) get sequential sacrificial bridges
  (`bridge.sacrificial.first`, `.second`).
- Design bridges in: straight spans between parallel walls, under
  `bridge.max.span` (10 mm) hidden, `bridge.conservative.span` (5 mm) on show
  or mating faces.
- Use angled brackets and gussets instead of flat shelves.
- Grow islands from the body instead of starting them on supports
  (FDM-OVH-11).
- Where something must still overhang: reorient, split (step 10), or model the
  support in CAD: a fin with flat tines, a thumbtack, a pointed tower or a
  comb, gap `support.designed.gap` (0.2 mm), tines `support.designed.tine.*`.
  These are sized for the 0.4 mm nozzle and never scaled with the part
  ([FDM rules](references/fdm-rules.md) FDM-SUP-8 to -13). Generated supports
  only as the decision table allows.

### 5. Holes, pins and fits

- For every fit, pick the fit class from
  [fits and clearances](references/fits-and-clearances.md) section 2 (press,
  snug, sliding, free, print in place, snap body), apply it per side to every
  wall of the joint, and state the one-line rule:

  > Clearance (sliding): 0.20 mm per side = 0.40 mm diametral. Hole for the
  > 8.00 mm shaft modeled at 8.40 mm. Slicer XY hole compensation assumed 0.

  A lid has two walls per axis: a 60.00 x 40.00 opening takes a 59.60 x 39.60
  lip. Add the material's `clearance.adjust.side` (PETG +0.05, ABS and ASA
  +0.1).
- A hole that must finish at a stated size (screw clearance, insert) is the
  stated size plus `hole.compensation.diametral` (0.2 diametral). Do not add it
  on top of a fit clearance, and do not also set slicer hole compensation.
- Minimum hole `hole.min.diameter` (2 mm); minimum pin `pin.min.diameter`
  (1.8 mm, perimeters only under 5 mm: fillet the base, print loaded pins lying
  down). Export holes with at least 32 facets.
- Holes whose axis crosses layer lines split under a wedging screw: reinforce
  ([FDM rules](references/fdm-rules.md) FDM-ORI-6) or reorient.
- Prefer fit-tolerant features (tapers, lead-in chamfers, crush ribs, grip
  fins) over exact diameters.
- Locate with fins in slots, or cone pins, not printed cylindrical pins, and
  use at most two locating features
  ([fits and clearances](references/fits-and-clearances.md) section 11).

### 6. Embedded hardware

Use [hardware](references/hardware.md) for geometry:

- **Heat-set inserts** for threads that are serviced or under M5: hole
  `insert.mN.hole` (datasheet finished hole + 0.2 diametral), depth
  `insert.mN.depth`, wall `insert.mN.wall`, straight hole, insert on the face
  away from the mating part. The wall must print as perimeters or a solid
  modifier: insert joints fail where the perimeters separate from the infill.
  For a joint that must outlast the insert, thicken it (M5: about 3.6 mm,
  `insert.m5.wall.full_strength`, measured in PLA). In PETG, PC and ABS prefer
  self-tapping inserts or deeper engagement. Never torque a screw in a print
  to the metal table value ([hardware](references/hardware.md) sections 1 and
  2.4).
- **Captured nuts:** pocket across flats of the measured nut +
  `nut.pocket.clearance.af` (0.3 diametral), a flat parallel to the bed; a
  bottom pocket opposite the load is about twice as strong as a side slot.
  Square nuts are preferred over hex in side slots and embeds.
- **Magnets:** press pocket + `magnet.pocket.press.diametral` (printed over
  after a pause) or glue pocket + `magnet.pocket.glue.diametral` (after
  printing); enclosed depth + 0.4, cover `magnet.cover.layers` (2 layers);
  polarity written down; N grades limited to about 80 C. Where only one side
  must be a magnet, pair it with steel (a ball or a screw head): no polarity
  to get wrong ([hardware](references/hardware.md) section 4).
- **Pause and embed** (magnets, nuts, weights, bearings): a closed cavity with
  press clearance, depth + 0.2 to 0.5, a bridged or 45 deg roof; the pause goes
  before the first layer that covers it. A person performs the pause and the
  insertion.
- **Screws straight into plastic:** only for joints opened a few times, about
  1 Nm on M3; pilot `screw.selftap.m3.pilot` (2.7 for M3).
- **Screw heads clamp solid material:** counterbore or countersink so the web
  under the head is walls and skins, not infill (FDM-STR-12).
- **Snap fits, hinges, flexures, threads, split-part joints:** the strain
  limits, thicknesses and connector rules in the same file. Flexure leaves
  (practitioner values, no fatigue data): at least `flexure.leaf.min` (0.5 mm)
  at the center, tapering to `flexure.leaf.root` (1.0 mm) at filleted roots,
  in whole line widths; prove with a coupon. No modeled threads under
  `thread.min.modeled` (M5) or in horizontal holes; prefer M6 and up (the
  horizontal-hole rule is disputed by one source until coupon C20 decides).

### 7. Material-specific adjustments

Confirm the material against the part's environment with
[materials](references/materials.md) sections 4 and 7: leave PLA when the part
is over about 45 to 50 C, outdoors, preloaded, impact-loaded or flexing past 1
percent strain. Then apply the material's changes: PETG about 1.12x section
depth for PLA's stiffness and +0.05 per side clearance; ABS and ASA rounded
footprints, brims, +0.1 per side, Z strength 0.5 and 0.3 on open printers;
nylon designed to wet properties. For a compliant mechanism state the trade-off
plainly: PETG takes more strain and does not creep shut like PLA, but it is
less stiff, strings, and is somewhat harder to print; PLA is stronger in
stress-limited fatigue (section 5 of the same file). Data sheets measured on
annealed or hot-chamber specimens overstate Z strength; use the conservative
values.

### 8. Bed contact, first layer and warping

- Chamfer every bed-contact edge (`chamfer.bed.edge`, 0.5 mm x 45 deg; 1 mm for
  an unknown printer), always on mating faces that touch the bed.
- Round vertical corners of large footprints (`corner.bed.radius.large`, 4 mm),
  especially in ABS, ASA, PC, nylon, PP.
- No text, logos or small islands on the first layer.
- For warping materials: brim regions or designed mouse ears (one layer thick),
  no long thin flat plates.

### 9. Small features and text

- Standalone features at least `feature.min.size` (1.8 mm); gaps meant to stay
  open at least `feature.min.gap` (0.5 mm).
- Text: bold sans-serif, at least `text.min.height` (5 mm), stroke
  `text.min.stroke` (0.9), raised `text.emboss.height` (0.6) or engraved
  `text.engrave.depth` (0.8); on vertical walls or top faces. On a vertical
  wall keep both at or under 0.5 (`text.sidewall.emboss.max`,
  `text.sidewall.engrave.max`); on a top face engrave rather than emboss.
- Give cosmetic parts a seam home (an inside vertical corner or a groove on a
  hidden side); avoid shallow domes and smooth cylinders on show faces.

### 10. Split for the build volume and join

Split when the part exceeds the build volume, when no orientation suits its
loads, or when one piece would need supports that a cut removes. Cut where the
seam is least visible and away from high stress, put each cut face on the bed,
add alignment (metal dowel pins, tongue and groove, keys) and a joint (inserts
and screws, snaps, or glue: CA for PLA, epoxy for PETG and ABS), and budget the
tolerance stack across every joint (worst case;
[fits and clearances](references/fits-and-clearances.md) section 7).

### 11. Pick coupons or a printed slice

For every value that is not yet proven on this printer, material and nozzle,
pick a coupon from [test coupons](references/test-coupons.md) section 2, or
specify a printed slice of the critical interface (section 3). List them with
the profile value each one proves. A person prints them; when a result comes
back, record it as a `proven` (or `failed`) value through **dfm** and adjust
the model per side.

### 12. Write down the print assumptions

Write the assumptions the part depends on in the `notes` of the part's DFM
layer (the **dfm** skill's part layer, where its waivers also live), and as
comments beside the design values when the part has a `cad.config.ts`. Every
deliberate departure from a rule (a support, a thin wall, a long bridge) is a
waiver in the same layer, with the rule id from
[FDM rules](references/fdm-rules.md):

```text
Print assumptions: <part>
  mode:                single printer | production run (quantity, farm or service)
  process / printer:   FDM, <printer model> (or "unknown, 256 mm bed assumed")
  nozzle / line / layer: 0.4 / 0.45 / 0.2 mm; first layer 0.2
  material:            PLA (or ..., with why)
  orientation:         <face> on the bed, <axis> up; exported in this orientation
  plate:               textured PEI (bottom face is cosmetic) | smooth PEI
  supports:            none | where, and why the decision table allows it
  walls / infill:      3 perimeters, <n>% infill; solid around inserts
  slicer compensation: XY hole 0, XY contour 0, elephant's foot <value>
  pauses:              none | layer <n> (Z <mm>): insert <item>, polarity <face out>
  clearances applied:  <one line per fit, per side and diametral>
  coupons or slice:    <which, and the value each proves>
  assumed, not stated: <list>
```

Then hand the part to **dfm-review**.

## Decision points

| Decision | Rule |
| --- | --- |
| Supports acceptable | Only for a large horizontal hole that must be crisp, a hidden or later-machined face, a one-off prototype, or a feature that cannot be chamfered, reoriented or split without losing function. Never on a mating or cosmetic face. With a soluble or breakaway interface (multi-material printer) the underside can be clean. Record it as a waiver (rule FDM-SUP-1) with where and why. |
| Split a part | When it exceeds the build volume; when arms load in conflicting directions and a 45 deg tilt does not fix it; when one piece needs supports that a cut removes; when a cosmetic face and a bed face conflict. Not across a high-stress region. |
| Drill or ream instead of printing a hole to size | Bearing seats, dowel locations, press fits on metal parts, and any diameter tighter than plus or minus 0.2 mm: model 0.2 to 0.3 undersize and drill or ream. Also any hole under 2 mm. |
| Insert, captured nut or screw into plastic | Serviced joint, threads under M5, or a clean flush look: heat-set insert in PLA, self-tapping insert (or deeper engagement) in PETG, PC or ABS, always in a boss of solid perimeters. Highest pull-out or high torque: captured nut in a bottom pocket (side slot if loading must be easy, square nut preferred). Rarely opened, low torque, room temperature: screw into plastic, never a wood or coarse plastic-thread screw into sparse infill. Printed threads only where an insert one size smaller would not do. PLA under sustained clamp load or warm: insert in PETG, PC, ASA or ABS, or a through-bolt. |
| Embed with a pause or not | Pause only when the item must be fully enclosed; otherwise an open pocket with glue or a press, or a side slot, so the printer is not held idle. A person performs the pause. |
| Another printing process is the better answer | Walls or features under one line width that must stay; tolerances tighter than plus or minus 0.2 mm without machining; smooth cosmetic surfaces with fine detail (SLA, PolyJet); complex overhangs or internal channels in tough nylon (SLS, MJF); metal. Say so and hand back to **dfm**; do not apply FDM values to the other process. |
| Change the material | Over about 45 to 50 C, outdoors, sustained preload, impact, or repeated flexing over about 1 percent strain: per [materials](references/materials.md) section 7. The user's named material wins; state the trade-off. |
| Prove with a coupon first | Press fits, print in place, snap fits, living hinges, pause-and-embed, any value tagged UNVERIFIED, SNIPPET or `assumption`, a different printer, nozzle or material than the one a value was proven on, a long or costly print, or a design past a typical limit ([test coupons](references/test-coupons.md) section 4). |
| Which value wins | The user's stated value always; then a `proven` value from a coupon on this printer; then the profile's `house` and `typical` values; the unknown-printer values when the printer is unknown. |
| Production run or single printer | Printed in quantity, on an automated farm, or by a print service from one file: production mode per [production runs](references/production-runs.md) (bed contact shaped for release, brims, rafts and supports modeled in CAD, no per-job settings, the cautious `wall.min.cautious` and landing values, coupons C22 to C26 and a sample batch). Otherwise the steps above as written. Record the mode in the print assumptions. |

## Verification

```bash
npx protoboard check --format json   # the model still resolves with 0 errors; this checks the model, not printability
```

Then check by reading the geometry against the profile:

- every wall is at least `wall.min` and a whole multiple of `wall.unit` where it
  carries load;
- no overhang past `overhang.max.angle` and no bridge past `bridge.max.span`
  without a recorded decision;
- every fit has its one-line clearance statement with per-side and diametral
  values and the slicer assumption;
- every bed-contact edge is chamfered; no first-layer text;
- the print assumptions block is complete and lists what was assumed.

Then **dfm-review**, by an agent that did not do the design. A passing review
is not a print: a person prints the coupons, slice and part.

## Failure handling

Print failures traced back to design causes:

| Symptom | Design cause | Do |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Thin wall missing or blobby | Wall under one line width (for example 0.2 mm at a 0.4 mm nozzle) | Thicken to 1 or 2 line widths, or change process |
| Gaps or zigzag fill inside a wall | Wall between whole multiples of line width | Round to a multiple of `wall.unit` |
| Drooping or stringy underside | Overhang past 45 deg, a downward fillet, or a curved bridge | Chamfer, reorient, or teardrop; straighten the bridge |
| Horizontal hole oval, flat or tight on top | No teardrop; top bridge sagged | Teardrop or roof the top; or drill |
| Counterbore from below is messy | Circle printed over air | Sequential sacrificial bridges |
| Shaft or pin will not go in | Clearance applied once instead of per side, or hole shrink ignored | Recompute per side and diametral; prove with C1 |
| Fit sloppy | Clearance applied twice, or slicer hole compensation stacked on a modeled offset | Recompute; set slicer compensation to 0 and record it |
| Lid jams only at the bottom | Elephant's foot on a mating edge at the bed | Chamfer the bed edge, or flip the part |
| Part cracks along a layer line | Load across layers | Reorient (tilt at least 45 deg) or split; check `strength.z.fraction` |
| Screw boss splits | Hole axis across layers, thin wall, or wedging screw | Insert or nut, more wall, reinforcement slots, reorient |
| Insert spins, pulls out or leaves a burr | Hole too large, or too small, or wall too thin | Use the insert's datasheet hole + 0.2 diametral; prove with C9; thicken the boss |
| Boss cracks around an insert before it pulls out | Perimeter ring separated from the infill; wall printed as infill | More perimeters or a solid modifier around the boss (M5: about 3.6 mm) |
| Nut falls out or will not load | Pocket clearance wrong; across corners modeled | Model across flats + 0.3 diametral; prove with C10 |
| Magnet pulled out by the nozzle | Pocket too loose, or too shallow so the nozzle hits it | Press clearance, depth + 0.4; glue; prove with C11 |
| Pause landed too late, item covered | Pause on the covering layer's wrong side | Pause before the first covering layer; check the slicer preview; one layer early with a deep cavity |
| Corners lift, part warps | Sharp large-footprint corners, warping material, long flat plate | Round corners, brim or mouse ears, split, or change material |
| Edge-standing part detaches | Point or edge bed contact | Chamfer a flat contact pad, add a designed fin |
| Print-in-place joint fused | Gap too small, or seam blobs in the gap | Larger gap (C3); keep seams away from the joint |
| Snap arm cracks | Strain over the material limit, sharp root, beam built across layers | Longer or tapered beam, root fillet 0.5 t, print it lying in XY, or PETG |
| Text illegible | Too small, too thin, on the first layer or a shallow slope | 5 mm, 2 line widths, on a wall or top face |
| PLA part deformed in use | Heat over about 45 to 50 C, or sustained preload creep | PETG, ASA, PC per the materials guide; through-bolts with washers |
| Visible support scars on a show face | Supports accepted on a cosmetic face | Redesign the overhang; reorient |
| Designed support fused to the part, or fell away | Gap too small, or the support scaled with the part | `support.designed.gap` at the production nozzle; redraw, never scale; prove with C22 |
| Production part will not release or stains on the bed | Large solid bed contact | Ring, pocket or edge contact per [production runs](references/production-runs.md) section 2; prove with C25 |

Model-level problems the CLI reports are listed in **dfm**'s own failure
handling and its review in **dfm-review**.

## Hard rules

1. Never start, upload or send a print, and never operate a printer: a person
   prints coupons, slices and parts, and performs every pause and insertion.
2. Every clearance is stated per side and diametral with the arithmetic; a
   bare "0.2 mm clearance" is not a design value.
3. Every number carries its source or is labeled derived, assumption or
   unverified; never upgrade an UNVERIFIED or SNIPPET value to a fact.
4. These are recommendations: the user's stated values override them, and the
   agent says which values it assumed.
5. Do not apply FDM values to another printing process.
6. Never drop a specified feature to pass a rule; change the orientation, the
   material, the split or the process, and say so.
